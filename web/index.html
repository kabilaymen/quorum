A Raft cluster you can break

Quorum is a replicated key-value store I built on my own implementation of Raft, in Go. The five nodes below run that code, compiled to WebAssembly, in your browser. Stop them, cut them off the network or make messages get lost, and see how the cluster keeps its data consistent.

    How to read the diagram

    A line between two nodes means they exchanged messages in the last few milliseconds. Grey lines carry log entries and heartbeats, orange lines carry votes during an election, blue lines carry a snapshot to a node that fell too far behind.

    After every step the page checks Raft's two safety rules: there is never more than one leader in a term, and no two nodes ever store different entries at the same position of the log. The counter under the diagram shows how many checks have passed.

    The network, the disks and the clock are simulated here (one tick is 10 ms). The TCP transport and the write-ahead log on a real disk are not on this page: they ran in production, below.

    How it ran in production

    The full system, three nodes talking over TCP and writing a checksummed log to disk with fsync, ran on a small cloud VM from October to November 2026.
